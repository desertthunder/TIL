Thurman Munson spent all 11 of his Major League seasons with the New York Yankees. He
finished with a .292 batting average and 1,558 hits, won the 1970 American League Rookie
of the Year award, three Gold Gloves, and the 1976 AL MVP, and made seven All-Star teams.
That year, the Yankees named him their first captain since Lou Gehrig.[^1]

Munson batted .357 across 30 postseason games. He helped New York reach three straight
World Series from 1976 through 1978 and win the last two.[^2]

Away from baseball, Munson was a private husband and father. He learned to fly and bought
a jet so he could travel between New York and his family in Canton, Ohio, on off days.
He died at 32 on August 2, 1979, while practicing landings at Akron-Canton Airport.[^1]

The Yankees retired his No. 15, dedicated a Monument Park plaque to him, and preserved
his locker. On the 47th anniversary of his death, current Yankees catcher Austin Wells
added another tribute: custom gear bearing Munson's name and number, plus a shaved
mustache styled after his.[^3]
