The Indian Residential Schools Settlement Agreement (IRSSA) resolved the largest
class-action lawsuit in Canadian history. Former students brought claims against the
Canadian government and churches involved in running residential schools. The parties
reached the agreement in 2006, courts approved it in 2007, and implementation began on
September 19, 2007.[^1]

The agreement created five main forms of redress:

- a Common Experience Payment for eligible former students who had lived at a
  recognized residential school
- an Independent Assessment Process for claims of sexual or serious physical abuse
- funding for healing programs
- funding for commemorative projects
- the [Truth and Reconciliation Commission of Canada](/notes/2026/08/05/ntrc-ca/).[^2]

The two compensation programs addressed different harms. The Common Experience Payment
recognized the shared experience of living at a residential school, while the
Independent Assessment Process evaluated individual abuse claims.[^3]

Eligibility rules left some former students outside parts of the settlement. Day
Scholars, who attended residential schools but did not live at them, could use the
Independent Assessment Process but could not receive the Common Experience Payment.
They later pursued a separate class action and settlement.[^4]
