The Truth and Reconciliation Commission of Canada (TRC) was created through the
[Indian Residential Schools Settlement Agreement](/notes/2026/08/05/irssa-ca/). From
2008 to 2015, it documented the history and ongoing effects of the residential school
system and worked to inform Canadians about the experiences of Survivors, their
families, and their communities.

**Survivors** are Indigenous people who lived through the residential school system.
The schools separated children from their families and communities and suppressed their
languages, cultures, and identities. Many students also endured neglect and physical,
sexual, and emotional abuse.[^1]

The commission gathered nearly 7,000 video statements from Survivors and
**intergenerational Survivors**, their descendants and other relatives who continue to
live with the system's effects, along with millions of government and church records.[^2]

Its final report concluded that the residential school system amounted to cultural genocide.
Governments and churches separated Indigenous children from their families to break the
ties through which their cultures and identities were sustained.[^3]

The TRC issued [94 Calls to Action](https://nctr.ca/publications-and-reports/reports/#trc-reports) in 2015 to address the system's legacy in child welfare, education,
language and culture, health, and justice, as well as the responsibilities of governments,
churches, schools, media, businesses, and other institutions in reconciliation.[^3]

The commission ended its mandate in 2015, but described reconciliation as an ongoing
process of repairing relationships and changing institutions. Its records are now
held by the [National Centre for Truth and Reconciliation](https://nctr.ca), which
preserves Survivor testimony and supports continued research and education.[^1]
